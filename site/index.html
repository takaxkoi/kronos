Questions, answered.

Is this financial advice?

No. It is a research tool. Forecasts are estimates, they are often wrong, and the app shows its own track record so you can judge how much weight to give them.

Where does the data come from?

Public market prices, news feeds, SEC filings, published earnings dates, an economic calendar and public Reddit posts. Every page says where its numbers came from and when they were last updated.

How often does it update?

Prices and news refresh about every 30 minutes while the US market is open. The deeper work, such as grading calls and re-testing strategies, runs after each close.

Does it trade real money?

No. Every strategy runs on practice money with real prices. Nothing is bought or sold for you.

Why does it admit when it is wrong?

Because a forecast you cannot check is worth nothing. Each call is written down before the outcome is known and graded afterwards, next to what always guessing "up" would have scored.
